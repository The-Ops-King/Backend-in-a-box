# Tyler's voice — working guide

Built from his messages in this project plus the Fathom transcript of the Nelly Sudri
call, Oct 2 2026. Apply to anything client facing.

## Two registers, only one of them ships

**Written to me:** lowercase "i", typos left in (coppied, hastle, maange), sentences run
together with commas, opens with "OK so" / "Yeah" / "I guess", CAPS for emphasis.
This is the thinking register. **Never put it in a document.**

**Spoken on a sales call:** this is the model. Professional and still unmistakably him.

## The signature move

Chains of short "I'll" promises with plain verbs.

> "I'll take that. I'll clean it up. I'll make sure that all of the systems and
> operations and everything's in place, and then I'll continue to run it and manage it
> and make sure everything's good to go."

Verbs he actually uses: handle, clean up, run, manage, take care of, build, set up, watch,
fix, connect. No abstraction. When describing his own work, default to this structure.

## Rhythms to use

- **"So" to open an explanation.** Not filler. It's how he starts.
- **Short parallel declaratives.** "It needs marketing. It needs sales ops and it needs fulfillment."
- **"and then" chains** rather than subordinate clauses.
- **Hedge, then commit.** "So, yes and no. Building funnels, less so. What I'm a wizard at is systems and operations."
- **"really" and "super" as intensifiers**, used sparingly in writing. Not "exceptional," not "significant."
- **"kind of" and "a little bit of"** to soften.
- **Self deprecation on real gaps only**, never on the core skill. He'll say pages aren't his
  strength. He will not hedge on systems.

## Never write

- **Negative parallelism.** "They're genuinely different, not the same thing at three prices."
  He'd say "They're pretty different, so read all three."
- **Manufactured candor.** "I'm telling you this up front so there's no confusion in week
  three." He'd just say "Here's what I don't do."
- **Constructed punchlines.** "I'd rather give you a date I'll beat than one I'll apologize
  for." Too tidy. Nothing he says is that polished.
- **Ad copy.** "Predictable cost, no matter how big this gets." Say "Your cost stays the
  same no matter how big this gets."
- **Em dashes and en dashes.** Ever.
- **Aphorisms.** "X is the Y of Z" constructions.

## Test

Read it out loud. If it sounds like a line somebody workshopped, cut it. He talks in
complete thoughts that occasionally wander and he does not correct himself mid idiom.
He once said "I couldn't sell water to a whale or whatever the phrase is" and kept going.
That confidence is the tone, not polish.

## Explanation discipline

State what it does and why. Then stop.

Cut the third sentence that explains the second. Cut worked examples of the value
("on a $3,500 plan that's the difference between..."). Cut "That's the whole job" style
summaries. If a line reads as justification rather than description, it goes.

Also keep system descriptions mechanism-agnostic in client documents. Say "every outcome
gets logged," not "Fathom catches it and the closer gets a Slack message with two links."
Naming the exact mechanism locks the build in writing and makes it harder to change later.

Watch for "that's the difference between X and Y." It is one of the clearest AI tells
in this document family.
